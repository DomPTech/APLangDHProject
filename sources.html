Overview

Various primary and secondary sources that provide insight into the Tennessee Valley Authority's impact and legacy

Primary Documents

1. FDR’s Congressional Address on the TVA (1933)

Official Message / Political Archive

Roosevelt, Franklin D. "Message to Congress Suggesting the Tennessee Valley Authority." April 10, 1933. Presidential Papers of Franklin D. Roosevelt.

Annotation: This address outlines the federal vision for regional planning, framing the valley as a sick region needing centralized federal intervention. It establishes the dual nature of the TVA as a government entity with corporate flexibility.

2. William Wirt's Relocation Protest Letter (1938)

Personal Correspondence / Relocation File

Wirt, William. Letter to the Tennessee Valley Authority Land Acquisition Department. 1938. Hosted on Appalachia Bare.

Annotation: Documents the intimate, personal grief and local opposition to eminent domain, directly challenging the clean progress narratives of federal planners by comparing the forced removal of Appalachian mountain families to Native American displacements.

3. Lewis Hine TVA Relocation Photographs (1933)

Photographic Survey / National Archives

Hine, Lewis. "Relocation of families from the Norris reservoir area." Tennessee Valley Authority Records, National Archives.

Annotation: Commissioned by the TVA, Hine’s photographs capture "moving day" for agrarian families. While intended to document orderly progress, they serve as a stark visual primary record of the physical erasure of rural Appalachian homesteads.

Secondary Sources

1. TVA and the Dispossessed (Scholarly Book)

Sociological & Historical Study

McDonald, Michael J., and John Muldowney. TVA and the Dispossessed: The Resettlement of Population in the Norris Dam Area. Knoxville: University of Tennessee Press, 1982.

Annotation: A definitive sociological study analyzing the long-term economic and psychological effects of Norris Dam relocations. It provides the empirical data supporting our argument that local residents bore the cost of regional power.

2. Beck Cultural Exchange Center Digital Archives

Local History Portal

Beck Cultural Exchange Center. "Urban Renewal in Knoxville: The Erasure of The Bottom." Beck Cultural Exchange Center Archives, Knoxville, TN. beckcenter.net

Annotation: Documents the mid-century clearance of East Knoxville’s Black business districts. This archive demonstrates how the top-down planning precedents established by the TVA later justified urban renewal projects that displaced Black families.

3. Museum of Appalachia Archives

Material Culture & Historic Preservation

Museum of Appalachia. "The Mountaineers' Sacrifice & Renewal: The Flooding of Norris Basin." Norris, TN. knoxvillehistoryproject.org

Annotation: Houses relocated structures (e.g., the Arnwine Cabin) and oral histories of Norris Basin families. This preservation archive grounds our digital project in the physical, material realities of pre-dam Appalachian culture.
