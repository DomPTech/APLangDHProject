Overview

Various primary and secondary sources that provide insight into the Tennessee Valley Authority's impact and legacy

Primary Documents

1. Mattie Randolph Quote (1936)

Personal Interview

Randolph, Mattie. Statement as landowner in East Tennessee. 1936. Link

Annotation: This quote provides a personal account of a landowner's resistance to the TVA's land acquisition.

2. Letter from Lorena Hickok To Harry L. Hopkins (1934)

Personal Correspondence

Hickok, Lorena. "Letters from the Field: Reports on the State of the Nation," Florence, Alabama June 6, 1934. http://newdeal.feri.org/tva/tva04.htm

Annotation: This letter documents the optimistic perspective and progress narrative of those working on the New Deal initiatives.

3. FDR’s Congressional Address on the TVA (1933)

Official Message

Roosevelt, Franklin D. "Message to Congress Suggesting the Tennessee Valley Authority." April 10, 1933. Presidential Papers of Franklin D. Roosevelt.

Annotation: This address outlines the federal vision for regional planning, framing the valley as a region needing centralized intervention.

4. Lewis Hine TVA Relocation Photographs (1933)

Photographic Archive

Hine, Lewis. "Relocation of families from the Norris reservoir area." Tennessee Valley Authority Records, National Archives.

Annotation: These photographs capture "moving day" for agrarian families, serving as a primary visual record of their relocation.

Secondary Sources

1. The Lost Tennessee Town That Flooded

Article

"The Lost Tennessee Town That Flooded." My Family Travels. myfamilytravels.com

Annotation: This article recounts the specific impact and complete submergence of historic towns like Loyston.

2. TVA and the Dispossessed

Scholarly Book

McDonald, Michael J., and John Muldowney. TVA and the Dispossessed: The Resettlement of Population in the Norris Dam Area. Knoxville: University of Tennessee Press, 1982.

Annotation: This sociological study provides empirical data on the long-term economic and psychological effects of Norris Dam relocations.

3. Museum of Appalachia Archives

Historic Preservation

Museum of Appalachia. "The Mountaineers' Sacrifice & Renewal: The Flooding of Norris Basin." Norris, TN.

Annotation: This archive grounds research by offering oral histories and material artifacts from relocated Norris Basin families.
